How to use Volume 2
This document extends the Master Website Build Brief. It does not replace it.
Volume 1 defined what Bold Clicks is, the brand system, the launch sitemap, the homepage blueprint, the
service-page template, the technical SEO baseline, and the launch acceptance checklist. Volume 2 answers the
questions Volume 1 left open: how the site actually ranks in Austin, across Texas, and in the surrounding states;
what content gets produced, when, and by whom; and how the whole system runs itself so the owner never
has to wonder whether tracking, indexing, performance, or forms are working.
PRECEDENCE RULES
1. If Volume 1 and Volume 2 conflict on a brand, logo, or 'do not fabricate' rule, Volume 1 wins.
2. If they conflict on SEO strategy, automation, content cadence, or implementation detail, Volume 2 wins (it is the
more specific document).
3. Anything marked [OWNER] in either volume is a required input from the owner. Claude Code must create a
clearly labeled placeholder in the content model and list it in the final hand-off report - never invent it, never ship it as
visible copy.
4. Automation may draft, test, measure, and propose. Automation never publishes public content or changes
tracking without an approval step.
The goal, stated plainly
Bold Clicks intends to be the number-one paid-media agency in the country. Nobody ranks nationally on day one, and
a site that tries to rank everywhere at once usually ranks nowhere. So the ranking strategy is sequenced: own
Austin first (where the operators, the proof, and the business address are), then Texas (Dallas-Fort Worth, Houston,
San Antonio), then the surrounding states (Oklahoma, Louisiana, Arkansas, New Mexico), and only then compete
on national head terms. Each tier unlocks when the previous tier produces measurable proof - rankings, leads, and
case studies - that can be reused in the next tier.

18 / GEOGRAPHIC RANKING STRATEGY
Own Austin. Then own Texas.
Local and regional search for agency services is won with a small number of strong pages plus off-site
signals - not with city-page sprawl.
Why geography is the first fight
When someone in Texas searches 'PPC agency' or 'Google Ads management,' Google usually localizes the results:
a map pack of nearby agencies, then organic results that lean toward businesses with local relevance. National
agencies with huge authority still show up, but a local specialist with a verified Google Business Profile, consistent
citations, real reviews, and a page that speaks to the local market can outrank them for the localized version of the
query. That is the opening. Bold Clicks is in Austin, which is also one of the most competitive local markets in the
country for home services, med spas, dental, and legal - exactly the verticals Bold Clicks serves.

DOORWAY-PAGE REJECTION TEST (run in code review and in the monthly SEO audit)
A location page fails if any of these is true: (a) more than 60% of its body text is shared with another location page;
(b) it contains no figure, screenshot, client reference, or observation that could only be true for that market; (c) it
exists only to funnel to the same form with no unique buyer questions answered. Failing pages get noindex and a
rewrite ticket - they do not get published.

19 / COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE PROTOCOL
Never guess who you are competing with
The competitive set changes as the site moves through tiers. Automation keeps the list current;
humans decide what to do about it.
Volume 1 named four national agencies as pattern references. Those are not the competitors Bold Clicks meets on
an Austin SERP. The Austin and Texas competitive set will include local PPC shops, full-service agencies with strong
local SEO, and directory sites (Clutch, UpCity, Yelp) that rank for 'best PPC agency austin' themselves. This section
defines a script, not a static list, so the competitive picture is always current and never based on stale assumptions.
Monthly SERP scan (automated)
1.
For every query in the tracked set (section 20, priority P1 and P2, with and without geographic modifiers), fetch
the top 20 organic results and the map-pack entries using a SERP data provider [OWNER chooses:
DataForSEO, SerpApi, or Semrush API - the repo must support any of them behind one adapter].
2.
Store results in data/serp/YYYY-MM/ as JSON, committed to the repo so history is diffable.
3.
Classify each result domain: local agency, national agency, directory/aggregator, platform doc (Google),
publisher, other.
4.
Compute share of voice per query group and per geography tier.
5.
For any domain appearing in the top 10 for three or more P1 queries, run a page-level profile: title, H1, word
count, presence of case studies, offer type, form fields, schema types, estimated Core Web Vitals via the
PageSpeed Insights API.
6.
Produce docs/competitive/YYYY-MM.md: what changed since last month, new entrants, who lost positions, and
which competitor pages Bold Clicks' equivalents are weaker than (and on which dimension).
7.
Open a GitHub issue labeled 'competitive-gap' for each concrete, actionable gap. Include the evidence.

Cluster 6: Brand and navigational
'bold clicks', 'bold clicks agency', 'bold clicks austin', 'bold clicks reviews'. Homepage, About, and Case Studies own
these. Automation tracks brand-query impressions monthly as the leading indicator of Tier 4 readiness. Register
bold-clicks and boldclicks handles on every major platform at launch even if unused, to protect the brand SERP.
Keyword-to-page assignment rules

Each commercial page has exactly one primary term in the title tag and H1 family. Supporting terms appear in
H2s, body, FAQs, and internal anchors - naturally.

Geographic modifiers live on location pages. Service pages mention Austin and Texas in copy and schema but do
not chase 'austin' in their titles; that would cannibalize the Austin page.

Educational articles never target a term already owned by a commercial page. If an article starts ranking for a
commercial term, the automation flags it and the fix is an internal link plus a canonical decision - not a second
commercial page.

Rank tracking is by query group (cluster) and by page, not by a single vanity keyword.

FRESHNESS HONESTY RULE
Do not bump 'updated' dates, sitemap lastmod, or Article dateModified unless the content materially changed. Fake
freshness is detectable and erodes trust with both Google and buyers. The CI pipeline blocks a lastmod change on
any file whose content diff is under 5% unless the commit is tagged 'material-update'.
Pruning rules

A page with zero clicks and under 100 impressions for 6 months is a candidate for consolidation (301 to the
strongest related page) or improvement. Automation lists candidates quarterly; the owner decides.

Never delete a page that has external links pointing to it without a 301 to the most relevant replacement.

Thin drafts that were never approved are deleted from the branch after 90 days so the backlog stays honest.

23 / AUTOMATION ARCHITECTURE
The site runs itself. The owner approves, not
operates.
Design principle: automate observation, drafting, testing, and reporting completely; keep publishing
and tracking changes behind a one-click human approval.
Operating principles
1.
One inbox, one digest. The owner receives one weekly digest email/SMS (via GoHighLevel) that lists everything
needing a decision, plus immediate alerts only for outages, broken forms, or tracking failures. Nothing else pings
the owner.
2.
Every job is idempotent and logged. A job can run twice without doing damage, and every run writes a JSON
log to data/runs/ committed to the repo, so 'is it working?' is answerable from Git history.
3.
Automation proposes through pull requests. Content drafts, refresh rewrites, meta-description fixes,
internal-link suggestions, and schema corrections arrive as PRs with a preview deployment. Approving the PR is
the publish action.
4.
Guardrails are code, not policy. CI blocks fabricated numbers (see section 26 linter), placeholder text, lastmod
abuse, missing metadata, and accessibility regressions. If it is important, it is a failing check, not a reminder.
5.
Fail loudly, degrade gracefully. If a data provider is down, the job reports 'skipped: provider unavailable' rather
than silently producing an empty report that looks like good news.
6.
No silent dependencies on one person. All credentials live in GitHub Actions secrets or the hosting provider's
environment; the README documents how to rotate every one of them.

APPENDIX C
KPI glossary and definitions
Definitions are fixed here so reports, case studies, and benchmarks all mean the same thing.
Term
Definition used on bold-clicks.com
Lead
A form submission, tracked call over the qualifying duration, or booked appointment from a unique
contact, deduplicated within 30 days
Qualified lead
A lead the client (or Bold Clicks on the client's behalf) has marked as a fit in the CRM: right service,
right area, real intent
Booked call / appointment
A calendar event created by the lead or the intake team, tied to a lead_id
Closed customer
A CRM opportunity marked won, with value when known
Cost per lead (CPL)
Ad spend / leads, for a stated platform, campaign set, and date range
Cost per qualified lead
(CPQL)
Ad spend / qualified leads, same scope
CAC
Ad spend (plus management fee if stated) / closed customers
ROAS
Attributed revenue / ad spend; always stated with the attribution model and date range
Wasted spend
Spend on search terms, placements, or audiences that produced zero leads over a stated window
and were subsequently excluded
Message match
The ad's promise, keyword/audience, headline, proof, and CTA are consistent on the landing page
Core Web Vitals 'good'
At the 75th percentile of field data: LCP <= 2.5s, INP <= 200ms, CLS <= 0.1
Tier unlock
The measurable conditions in section 00 that permit expanding pages and targeting to the next
geography
